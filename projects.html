One design system for Tetra Pak’s product portfolio

In short

The Context
Four external teams were designing Tetra Pak’s products in isolation, leading to a disjointed user experience, un-reusable design assets, and mounting technical debt.

The Strategy
I pitched and co-led a centralized design system initiative over four years. Collaborating with the Lead UX Researcher, we aligned all four external teams under a unified governance structure and shared UI standards.

The Impact
The design system now forms the UI foundation of Tetra Pak® Factory OS™—scaling across five products and 31 user types.

My job was split between high-level alignment and deep hands-on.

01 High-level alignment

Governing the Ecosystem

I worked with Tetra Pak stakeholders to understand their operating needs at the time and how they wanted their products to evolve. I managed friction between the external design teams and set up operating rules so everyone could design together consistently.

02 Deep hands-on

Leading AI adoption

I introduced AI into the DS workflow and expanded its role as the tools matured, from speeding up documentation, to creating communication assets, to analysing research data for new patterns such as dashboard guidelines for developers.

Design Ops for Complex Product Ecosystem

Design ops is how design works across product teams: it makes room to explore new ways of working, while protecting delivery and cost.

In short

The Context
More than 15 designers, in several external teams, were designing five Tetra Pak products in silos. Each team worked its own way, nothing was documented in one place, and no one had a full view of where each product stood.

What I did
I saw that the design system would only be sustainable if the way teams worked around it changed too. As Design Director, I defined and rolled out one operating model: a standard handoff from design to development, one shared Figma environment, and a contribution model for every change.

The Impact
Teams changed without the work starting over. Every product’s design status became visible in one place. And because every component request went through one check, the cost of teams building in silos became visible and controllable: 21% of requests were solved without building anything new.
