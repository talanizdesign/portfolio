What I actually did

My job was split between high-level alignment and deep hands-on.

01 High-level alignment

Governing the Ecosystem

I worked with Tetra Pak stakeholders to understand their operating needs at the time and how they wanted their products to evolve. I managed friction between the external design teams and set up operating rules so everyone could design together consistently.

02 Deep hands-on

Building inside Figma & Confluence

I created components, mapped semantics across products, including colour for interactive states, and ran workshops with Tetra Pak stakeholders. I audited how efficiently teams were using their tools and which assets were worth creating for Tetra Pak's context. I built the documentation first in Figma, then designed the information architecture to move it into Confluence.

03 Innovation

Leading AI adoption

I introduced AI into the DS workflow and expanded its role as the tools matured, from speeding up documentation, to creating communication assets, to analysing research data for new patterns such as dashboard guidelines for developers.

The Context

In short

The Context
More than 15 designers, in several external teams, were designing five Tetra Pak products in silos. Each team worked its own way, nothing was documented in one place, and no one had a full view of where each product stood.

What I did
I saw that the design system would only be sustainable if the way teams worked around it changed too. As Design Director, I defined and rolled out one operating model: a standard handoff from design to development, one shared Figma environment, and a contribution model for every change.

The Impact
Teams changed without the work starting over. Every product’s design status became visible in one place. And because every component request went through one check, the cost of teams building in silos became visible and controllable: in 2025, 16% of requests were solved without building anything new.

Datalogic Self-Shopping App

Scan, pay and go: a self-shopping app for supermarkets

Role
Design Director
~4 weeks
Scope
Product design
Information architecture
Prototype & handoff
Industry
Retail technology
Type
Mobile app

In short

The Context
Datalogic wanted to bring self-shopping to shoppers’ own smartphones: scan products while shopping, see the running total and offers, and skip the checkout queue. It was a new product idea, with one month to design it and no user data to start from.

The Strategy
With no user data, we went to the store. The team used competitors’ self-shopping solutions in supermarkets and went through the whole shopping trip, then combined what we saw with Datalogic’s technical features into one product intelligence base. Product managers and developers were involved from the start.

The Impact
In four weeks we delivered the information architecture, pixel-perfect screens, an interactive prototype and a developer handoff that was feasible to build and met business needs. The app shipped and is now part of Datalogic’s retail product portfolio.
