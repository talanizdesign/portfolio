Building an enterprise design system from zero across 9 products

The challenge

Design a scalable design system for a family of products that supports the company's end-to-end production process and serves a business with $12B in revenue.

The ecosystem spans three interfaces: mobile, web, and HMI, each needing consistency while adapting to different user needs and operational contexts.

The system was built as the foundation for the company's broader digital ecosystem, guided by brand guidelines and communication design principles.

Results

2023CreationDesign Ops and full Design System built and published.
2024AdoptionDesign system adopted across 5 products; DS capability trained into the internal team.
2025EvolutionProduct family unified under one system with variable-based theming.
2025ExpansionExpansion of the DS to other modes and dashboards.

Context

Users

4 External Vendors for Design Services

Main problems

Technical debt: designer turnover created constant rework cycles. Developers absorbed the cost — not designers. The same problems were solved from scratch with every rotation.

Design bankruptcy: zero reusability. Capital invested in assets that couldn't compound across the portfolio.

Inconsistent UX: each external vendor interpreted the brand guidelines in its own way, resulting in fragmented behaviour, visual language, and interaction logic across the ecosystem.

Deliverables

  • Component libraries
  • Styles
  • Variables
  • Documentation
  • Governance model
  • Adoption strategies

My role

  • Design System Lead, responsible for leading the design system team across the full lifecycle of the system.
  • Built training programs for new designers joining the team.
  • Created enablement frameworks that helped design managers run design system projects independently.

Awarded Red Dot Best of the Best 2024

The challenge

Design and deliver a next-generation HMI that improves usability, lowers the risk of operator error, and creates a more intuitive product experience.

Results

  • Awarded Red Dot "Best of the Best" 2024 in the Design Concept category, recognizing innovation and clarity in complex industrial systems.
  • Delivered a fully redesigned HMI that simplified user workflows and enhanced safety, usability, and machine interaction.

Context

IMS Technologies, a global leader in industrial automation, aimed to reimagine its Human-Machine Interface (HMI) for a new generation of highly complex, data-driven machines. The challenge was to design an HMI that could simplify machine control while reflecting the company's forward-thinking product strategy and customer-centric approach.

The approach I applied

01DiscoveryComprehensive research covering market trends, user needs, and internal business goals. On-site fieldwork with clients from China and Italy, stakeholder interviews, and contextual inquiry to align the HMI with operational workflows and strategic positioning.
02Product ConceptTranslated insights into a clear product vision, defining key features and visual concept directions; synthesized findings into a cohesive value proposition balancing usability, brand, and technical feasibility.
03Product DesignIterative co-design sessions with the client developed wireframes, interaction flows, and pixel-perfect screens; assembled the right expertise, applied appropriate methodologies, and evolved internal leadership capabilities.

My role

  • Led a multidisciplinary team delivering the full HMI experience, from research through design handoff to development.
  • Directed international user research, including on-site studies with clients in China, to uncover real-world needs in operational contexts.
  • Defined product features and shaped the concept direction to align user needs with business goals.
  • Ensured design decisions balanced user insight, technical feasibility, and strategic priorities.

Design Operations for a Complex Product Ecosystem

The challenge

Aligned multiple design teams around a shared product vision and operating model, while enabling each product to evolve independently within a common system.

Results

Process
  • Defined the end-to-end design process, including dev hand-off, change requests, and design system publication.
Figma Infrastructure
  • Created a unified Figma environment where all vendors work.
  • Built a shared setup that supports consistency and scalable collaboration.
Governance
  • Unified multiple design teams under a shared Design Ops framework, establishing consistent ways of working while preserving local autonomy.
  • Improved alignment between design, product, and engineering, reducing delivery friction and strengthening internal ownership.
  • Enabled products to feel like part of one coherent ecosystem.

Context

A multinational organization with multiple digital products was operating with several internal and external design teams working independently.

This led to fragmented user experiences, inconsistent design decisions, and products that felt disconnected, even though they belonged to the same ecosystem.

Each team optimized locally, but without a shared way of working, common design language, or clear ownership, design became harder to scale and the overall portfolio lost coherence.

The approach

01DiscoveryAssessed how teams were working independently and identified points of fragmentation across products and disciplines.
02FrameworkDefined a shared Design Operations framework covering roles, responsibilities, decision-making, and collaboration models.
03WorkflowsEstablished common workflows and rituals to align design, product, and engineering without introducing unnecessary bureaucracy.
04StandardsIntroduced shared principles and standards to support coherence across products, while allowing teams to adapt locally.
05AdoptionEmbedded these practices into day-to-day delivery to ensure adoption and long-term sustainability.

My role

As Design Director, I led the rollout of Design Operations across teams and products, partnering with design leadership, product owners, engineers, and external vendors to align on operating principles, roles, and delivery practices. My focus was on building scalable structures that enabled effective collaboration across teams, rather than relying on project-by-project coordination.

An emotional inventory for the digital products you use

Awa is a self-initiated product concept building on my published research, Emotion-Driven Innovation (Springer, 2024). The prototype shown is exploratory and represents work in progress. Awa — including its concept, name, and design — is the intellectual property of Teresa Alaniz. © 2026 Teresa Alaniz. All rights reserved.

The Idea

Awa is a personal inventory that tracks emotional experiences with digital products, not functional use. Many times we have products that are completely functional, and still we don't use them.

The Origin

Awa rises from Emotion-Driven Innovation (E-DI), the methodology introduced in my PhD research and published in Emotion-Driven Innovation (Springer, 2024).

It takes the knowledge developed through that research and applies it toward a different end: understanding the emotional landscape of digital products themselves, rather than designing a single product's experience.

How it works

Each time someone logs a check-in for an app or subscription, Awa builds a private, longitudinal record — not just the first impression, but whether that feeling has held steady or faded months later.

Value for the User

That record becomes evidence for two decisions people rarely have real data for:

  • Whether to start a new subscription, based on their own history with similar tools.
  • Whether to cancel one they already have, based on how it's actually made them feel over time — not a guess made in the moment.
